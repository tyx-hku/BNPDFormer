discrete manufacturing system 
Daoyuan Liu, Yu Guo, Shaohua Huang *, Shengbo Wang, Tao Wu 
College of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering, Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Nanjing 210016, China   
A R T I C L E I N F O  
Keywords: 
Production bottleneck prediction 
Long and short-term memory network 
Dual attention mechanism 
Dynamic update 
Discrete manufacturing system 
A B S T R A C T  
In the complex discrete manufacturing system (DMS), the production bottleneck shifts in space as time goes on 
and constrains operational efficiency. Accurate proactive production bottleneck prediction provides a reliable 
basis for dynamic production decisions and helps to improve management timeliness and production efficiency. 
According to the production characteristics of DMS and the relationship between supply and demand, the pro­
duction bottleneck is given a new quantification. A long and short-term memory network (LSTM) with dual 
attention mechanism and a dynamic updating method for the source model are proposed to predict production 
bottlenecks accurately. Firstly, feature and state attention mechanisms are designed to improve the feature 
extraction and prediction ability of LSTM. Secondly, as the applicability of the prediction model gradually de­
clines over time, sliding time windows and fast Hoeffding concept detection are combined to trigger the update 
of model parameters. Then a competitive strategy is explored to choose the source model that is the most suitable 
for the current data distribution in the model library. Model-based transfer learning is adopted to update the 
source model parameters, making the prediction model highly adaptive. Subsequently, an elimination strategy is 
set to update the model library to ensure its timeliness. Finally, experiments demonstrate that the proposed 
method is effective in bottleneck prediction and superior to other methods.   
1. Introduction 
With diversified customer demands and fierce market competition, 
manufacturing industries put higher requirements to enhance the 
management level and production efficiency of complex discrete 
manufacturing systems (DMS) [1,2]. All the manufacturing units 
determine the production efficiency. If one is weak, overall production 
efficiency is limited [3]. Production bottleneck refers to the 
manufacturing unit that most restricts the production efficiency of a 
manufacturing system [4–6]. The theory of constraints emphasizes that 
a manufacturing system can effectively improve its productivity after 
eliminating production bottlenecks [7,8], similar to Cannikin law [9]. 
Hence, it is crucial to identify current production bottlenecks and pre­
dict future ones, which assist in locating the problem that leads to low 
production efficiency and providing the basis for production decision- 
making. 
In a DMS, the machining process includes a series of discontinuous 
procedures. The details are as follows: Work-in-process (WIP) is trans­
ported from the previous station to the in-buffer of the current station to 
wait for processing. Then, WIP is processed and then transported to the 
next station. Hence, a station is similar to a middleman and needs to 
respond to the supply and demand of other stations in time. Otherwise, it 
will delay the production schedule and become the bottleneck. A new 
bottleneck indicator considering supply–demand relationships between 
stations needs to be defined. 
Bottleneck prediction allows managers to know which station is the 
most restrictive to production efficiency in advance and provide a basis 
for timely adjustment of production plans and formulation of production 
strategies. The big data-driven prediction method does not require un­
derstanding the complex operational logic or building multi-rule 
empirical models [10,11], which is suit for bottleneck prediction. 
With the help of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and 
manufacturing execution system (MES), the real-time manufacturing 
data, such as work-in-process status, equipment operation information, 
and production task composition, are more numerous and accurate [12]. 
Because of stochastic disturbances, different production tasks, and 
degraded equipment performance, the bottleneck indicator changes, the 
bottleneck unit shifts in space over time [13], and the production data

Since the production bottleneck has variability and time-series 
characteristics [14], an autoregressive moving average (ARMA) model 
was adopted to predict it. As the manufacturing data increase, a more 
robust feature extractor is needed to refine the knowledge hidden in the 
data [15]. Deep learning becomes a powerful helper. For example, Fang 
et al. [16] verified the validity of the parallel gated recurrent units 
(GRU) network for shifting lateness production bottleneck prediction. 
However, input features and states have different associations with the 
prediction targets for the time series problem. To fully mine temporal 
correlation in production bottleneck, feature and state attention should 
be considered. As the data distribution changes over time and the pre­
diction model trained from historical data is time-sensitive, the model 
parameters should be updated to maintain the prediction accuracy. This 
study aims to design a high-accuracy and robust applicability model to 
predict production bottlenecks. The main contributions are summarized 
as follows.  
(1) According to the supply–demand relationship between stations, a 
new production bottleneck indicator describing the balance of 
DMS is defined. Based on the actual operation condition of the 
DMS and the particularity of production bottlenecks, the data 
features regarding bottleneck prediction are analyzed.  
(2) To improve the accuracy of production bottleneck prediction, a 
long and short-term memory network with dual attention 
mechanism (LSTM-DAM) is proposed. The feature attention 
mechanism allows different features to play different roles in the 
training stage. The state attention mechanism is served to fuse 
multiple state features and make full use of the knowledge 
embedded in temporal data.  
(3) To address the problem of decreasing prediction accuracy over 
time, a dynamic updating method for the prediction model is 
proposed. The competitive and elimination strategy are proposed 
to select an appropriate source model from the model library and 
ensure the timeliness of the model library. Meanwhile, the 
concept drift detection and transfer learning are adopted to 
trigger and execute the update of the source model.

ducts a brief literature review on production bottleneck quantification 
and its prediction methods. Section 3 defines research questions and 
discusses the influencing factors. In the next section, the establishment 
of LSTM-DAM and its updating process are described in detail. In the 
subsequent section, several numerical experiments are presented to 
certify the accuracy and applicability of the proposed method. Finally, 
the conclusion and future research are summarized in section 6. 
2. Literature review 
2.1. Production bottleneck definition 
At present, many methods exist to quantify production bottlenecks. 
Hopp et al. [17] observed production bottlenecks by machine utiliza­
tion, where the station with the maximum utilization was denoted as the 
station with the minimum effective productivity. Machine utilization is 
an average value, and it gives us little advice on how the bottleneck 
station affects our production system [18]. In serial lines, some re­
searchers used the starvation and blockage of a station to describe 
production bottlenecks, such as arrow [19], inactive period [20], or 
turning point [21]. Further, Kalir et al. [22] and Roser et al. [23] used 
station inter-departure time variance and bottleneck walk to reflect 
production bottlenecks, respectively. As the research progressed, Wedel 
et al. [24] proposed an overall error efficiency method to determine the 
production bottlenecks of serial lines with parallel machines. However, 
there is no strict serial line in complex DMS, and the production process 
is discontinuous. The evaluation indexes above are either unable to 
monitor the real-time bottleneck dynamically or hard to reflect the real- 
world production bottlenecks comprehensively [25]. Other metrics are 
urgently needed to quantify production bottlenecks for DMS. Faget et al. 
[18] used queue waiting time to define production bottlenecks in 
discrete event systems. Deng et al. [26] integrated production load, 
utilization, and average buffer length to determine production bottle­
necks of flexible manufacturing systems. Fang et al. [16] studied the 
lateness shifting bottleneck by the relative proportion of relative late­
ness. Since only the production status of the current station is involved, 
the bottleneck indicators above do not consider the relationship be­
tween upstream and downstream. Each station should be treated as an 
intermediate link in production. If a station executes the production plan 
typically, on the one hand, it will quickly process the tasks assigned to 
this station; on the other hand, it will respond to the task demands from 
other stations in time. By defining production bottlenecks based on the 
supply and demand relationship, the following information is received: 
whether the production capacity of one station is suitable for DMS and 
how the bottleneck station affects the operation of the whole production 
system. 
2.2. Production bottleneck prediction 
Some literature is related to long-term and short-term bottleneck 
detection [27], which is to discover the bottleneck fluctuation patterns 
and analyze which station most affects the regular production on pre­
vious days. Others are concerned about real-time production bottleneck 
identification and future production bottleneck prediction. The latter 
provides a basis to make proactive decisions to weaken the negative 
impact of bottlenecks and improve production efficiency. Li et al. [28] 
and Subramaniyan et al. [29] used ARMA to predict machine blockage, 
starvation, and active periods. Deng et al. [26] and Huang et al. [30] 
proposed an adaptive network based fuzzy influence system (ANFIS) 
and deep neural network (DNN) to predict the future bottleneck index. 
Lai et al. [31] predicted the bottlenecks of an underbody assembly line 
by LSTM, and Fang et al. [16] put forward a parallel GRU network to 
forecast the future bottlenecks, both of which proved recurrent neural 
networks (RNN) are more effective for bottleneck prediction compared 
with DNN. 
In addition, RNN has been applied in other fields. Du et al. [32] used 
LSTM to predict daily urban water demand. Jin et al. [33] predicted 
energy consumption accurately through parallel LSTM neural networks. 
Others have also studied the attention mechanism. For example, Xiang 
et al. [34] used SCADA data and solved fault detection of wind turbines 
based on a convolutional neural network (CNN) and LSTM with atten­
tion mechanism. Du [35] proposed an attention-based LSTM network to 
forecast for cointegrated stocks and proved that it had a good applica­
tion effect. Zeng et al. [36] realized the well logging prediction through 
the attention mechanism based on GRU. The above proved that LSTM 
has advantages in mining time-series information, and the attention 
mechanism could improve the fitting ability. 
Although the above considered the time-series evolution of the 
production bottlenecks and used LSTM or GRU to predict them, they 
seldom consider feature attention and state attention mechanism 
simultaneously. In streaming data analysis, concept drift often occurs. 
Tian et al. [37] proposed the Bernstein inequality for detecting concept 
drift, which was used to trigger online learning to predict lithium-ion 
battery health. Mehmood et al. [38] discussed concept drift adaptation 
techniques and proved that updating the prediction model for real-world 
time series analysis was necessary. The distribution of manufacturing 
data will change over time, resulting in a decline in the applicability of 
the prediction model, which is rarely taken into account in the pro­
duction bottleneck prediction. Therefore, LSTM with dual attention 
mechanism and an update method of the source model is proposed to

3. Problem definition 
In a complex DMS, the dynamics of the production process, such as 
different production tasks and disturbance events, cause production 
bottlenecks to shift over time. The production bottleneck prediction 
refers to obtaining the bottleneck index of each unit and then finding the 
manufacturing unit that most restricts the efficient production in 
advance, which assists managers in adjusting the production plan and 
controlling the precise execution proactively. The principles below are 
followed in the DMS. (1) The DMS produces several types of parts, and 
the process route is determined. (2) Each station includes a machine tool 
for processing, an in-buffer and an out-buffer for temporarily storing 
WIPs. (3) The dispatcher determines the type and quantity of the next 
batch of products and their production time. 
As mentioned in Section 2, the existing quantitative indicators are 
insufficient to describe production bottlenecks for DMS. Hence, a new 
indicator named integrated capability (IC) is quantified. A station is 
regarded as an intermediate processor. It should respond to the supply of 
suppliers and the demand of demanders in time, achieving a balance 
between supply and demand throughout the supply chain. IC is defined 
as the ability to respond to supplies (SRA) and demands (DRA) of other 
stations. 
If the actual machining state of station i is close to ideal, that is, there 
is no WIP in the in-buffer, then the station is more responsive to the 
supplies from other stations. The difference between the actual and ideal 
state of a station is used to quantify supply responsiveness. Ideally, when 
WIPs arrive at the current station, processors immediately process the 
WIPs to respond to the supplies. However, the actual situation is that 
most WIPs need to enter the in-buffers to wait for processing. At this 
time, the longer the waiting time, the weaker the ability to respond to 
supplies. Therefore, the SRA of station i is expressed as:

4. Methodology 
The third part gives a new way to quantify production bottlenecks 
with the help of the supply and demand relationship, and the critical 
features for bottleneck prediction are obtained. Based on this, a 
comprehensive prediction model is established to predict production 
bottlenecks accurately. Firstly, WIP status describes the flow process of 
WIP, and machine information describes the operation of machines, 
which have different influences on the operation rules of the DMS. 
Meanwhile, the current status evolves from the historical status, and 
states have different temporal correlations with others. In bottleneck 
prediction, the contributions of different features and states should be 
distinguished. LSTM with dual attention mechanism is proposed to 
improve its predictability and applicability. After training, put the pre­
diction model into the model library. Then, the distribution of 
manufacturing data is dynamic, and concept drift will occur over time. 
In other words, the correlation between input and output changes, and 
the accuracy of the prediction model drops. Hence, Hoeffding inequality 
and sliding time windows are introduced to detect concept drift, which 
triggers the update of the prediction model. Finally, the competition 
strategy is proposed to select the appropriate source model from the 
model library. And source model parameters and the model library are 
updated based on transfer learning and elimination strategy to improve 
applicability and timeliness. The overall architecture is shown in Fig. 1. 
4.1. LSTM with dual attention mechanism

state, which illustrates that the changes in the manufacturing data are 
time series. LSTM is good at processing sequential data and extracting 
temporal characteristics through a recursive connection [39]. LSTM

inputs. Then the output cell state Ct and the hidden state Ht are 
outputted. Three stages are introduced into LSTM: forgetting, selective 
memory, and output. In the forgetting stage, it is mainly to retain 
valuable information and forget some additional useless ones. The

5. Experiment 
5.1. Experimental setting 
To analyze the performance of the proposed prediction model, the 
experiments were conducted in a typical machining workshop with 13 
stations, which are mainly responsible for processing eight types of 
products. RFID sensors are deployed in the buffer to collect WIP status. 
Machine information is obtained by Ethernet networks, Programmable 
Logic Controller, and so on. Production tasks are imported from the 
MES. Here, 25,246 pieces of data with 784 features were collected, with 
15,246 items employed to train the source model and the rest used for 
updating the model parameters and developing the model library. 70% 
of the data was used as the training set, 20% as the verification set, and 
the rest as the test set. The features have been described in Section 3, and 
a sample is shown in Table 1. The prediction model was coded by the 
PyTorch framework and ran on a computer with 24 GB GPU and an Intel 
Core i9. The hyperparameters of the prediction model were selected by 
several trials, as shown in Table 2. 784 represents the input dimension, 
which is the number of input features in the dataset. 300 indicates the 
number of LSTM nodes. 125–75 indicates the number of fully connection 
layer nodes after LSTM. 13 represents the number of output nodes in the 
neural network, corresponding to the bottleneck index of 13 stations. 
Normalizing all features and labels by min–max normalization can 
reduce the training difficulty. When the prediction model is applied in a 
DMS, the original features need to be normalized, and the output is anti- 
normalized to predict IC, reflecting the bottleneck level of stations in the 
future. 
5.2. Experimental comparison 
In order to verify that the method proposed in Section 4.1 is more 
suitable as the source model for bottleneck prediction, the proposed 
method is compared with others, including LSTM with feature attention 
mechanism (LSTM-FAM), LSTM with state attention mechanism (LSTM- 
SAM), and GRU with dual attention mechanism (GRU-DAM), LSTM, 
GRU, multi-layer perceptron (MLP), and Transformer. As described in 
the bottleneck definition, α is a value from 0 to 1 to adapt to different 
demands. Thus, α = 0.2, α = 0.5 and α = 0.8 are taken to verify the 
universality of the proposed method. With Root Mean Square Error 
(RMSE, calculated as (25)) as output, the convergence process of seven 
models on the verification set is recorded, as shown in Fig. 3. It clearly 
indicates that LSTM and GRU with the attention mechanism can 
converge to a smaller value, and the prediction performance can be 
improved if feature attention and state attention are added simulta­
neously. For Transformer, it can also converge to smaller values, but the 
convergence speed is slower. Meanwhile, compared with the time-series 
prediction methods, MLP is the worst, which proves that LSTM, GRU, or 
Transformer is more conducive to mining the time-series information 
and predicting the production bottleneck.

5.3. Example verification 
Three representative stations (8, 7, and 12) are selected from thirteen 
stations. The prediction error (MAE) of TLSTM-DAM is 0.014 for station 
8, 0.019 for station 7, and 0.029 for station 12. After anti-normalizing 
the predicted and actual values, the fitting results of TLSTM-DAM and 
TGRU-DAM are shown in Fig. 6. We can see the IC value describing the 
bottleneck of a station is not static, and its change is sequential. Both of 
them verified that the production bottleneck is dynamic and sequential. 
Further, the predicted values can realistically reflect the actual values, 
proving that the proposed method is suitable for mining production 
bottleneck shifting patterns in the future. IC is analyzed from two di­
mensions. Horizontally, if the IC of one station is the smallest, there is a 
manufacturing unit that restricts the regular operation of DMS, called 
the bottleneck unit. Vertically, the trend of IC is analyzed to determine 
the causes. When the IC changes periodically and the value is normal, 
the station runs at a standard takt time, as shown in Fig. 6(a) and (b). IC 
is too small in a short time, there is a high probability that materials are 
not transported in time, and new trigger rules should be set for trans­
portation. The other is that IC fluctuates unevenly and is partial to the 
negative axis, as shown in Fig. 6 (c). It is generally insufficient pro­
cessing capacity, and the task should be reallocated. The above illus­
trates IC could reflect the real-time production bottleneck and provide a 
reliable direction for analyzing the operation rules of a DMS and 
improving production efficiency. 
From the above experiments, the following conclusions are drawn. 
(1) LSTM with dual attention mechanism is proposed, which fully exerts 
the function of different features and states to improve the accuracy of 
production bottleneck prediction. (2) Through concept drift detection 
and the model updating method, the prediction model is dynamically 
adjusted, and the model library is automatically updated, both of which 
ensure the prediction model is always applicable. (3) Compared with 
other prediction models, the prediction value of TLSTM-DAM is closest 
to the actual value, which proves its availability and effectiveness for the 
DMS with nonlinear characteristics. (4) The bottleneck unit is proac­
tively identified by analyzing the future changes in IC, which provides a 
reference for production decision-making. 
6. Conclusion 
In this research, a novel bottleneck prediction model is constructed. 
By analyzing the complex DMS characteristics and combining the sup­
ply–demand relationship, IC is defined to quantify the production bot­
tlenecks, and features closely related to production bottlenecks are 
selected to predict production bottlenecks. Then feature and state 
attention mechanisms are introduced into LSTM to exploit the sparse 
value embedded in the data fully. Since the distribution of production 
data changes with time, the source prediction model’s applicability 
decreases, and its dynamic update is triggered by the concept drift 
detection method. In the updating stage, a competitive strategy is pre­
sented to determine the source model from the model library. Subse­
quently, transfer learning is introduced based on relevant incremental 
data to update the prediction model to improve its effectiveness. And an 
elimination strategy is designed to update the model library to ensure its 
timeliness. 25, 246 production data with 784 features are used to carry 
out numerical experiments, which proved the effectiveness of dual 
attention mechanism and model updating. 
Due to production bottleneck prediction and the information 
extracted from its analysis, the causes of the production bottlenecks are 
acquired. In future research, an appropriate time will be selected to 
relieve the negative impacts of the bottleneck unit. On this basis, the 
appropriate decision-making method will be adopted to solve produc­
tion bottlenecks, make the whole production process as balanced as 
possible, and ensure the timely delivery of orders.
