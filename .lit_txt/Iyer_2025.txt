ABSTRACT
Bottleneck detection and management plays a significant role in the context of Industry 4.0, 
wherein process chains have become more intricate. The dynamic nature of process chains shifts 
the bottleneck location, which requires an integrated methodology capable of identifying current 
as well as predicting future bottlenecks. The paper proposes a cognitive digital twin (CDT) with 
a novel explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) model. The proposed CDT is capable of (i) detecting 
existing bottlenecks, (ii) detecting data anomalies and process chain anomalies (iii) estimating 
shifting bottlenecks due to anomalies, (iv) predicting near future bottlenecks, and (v) the XAI model 
supports operational and strategic decision making. The usefulness of proposed CDT is demon­
strated and validated experimentally on an industry 4.0 compliant learning factory. The proposed 
novel CDT effectively addresses the process chain bottlenecks (existing, shifting, and future) while 
the XAI model enhances transparency and trustworthiness for practical implementation.

1. Introduction
The manufacturing industry has undergone a significant 
transformation with the introduction of industry 4.0 and 
the advancements in its enabling technologies. The 
data-driven evolution is fueling the sector’s resilience 
and ability to better manage disruptions. In order to 
meet the changing demands and expectations of cus­
tomers, as well as address environmental challenges, 
the industry is now focused on faster production, better 
customization capabilities, and sustainable manufactur­
ing processes. The process chain needs to be agile and 
reconfigurable to handle the variability [1]. However, 
the intricate manufacturing process chains often experi­
ence a decline in throughput as a result of several 
anomalies due to unexpected machine downtime, 
prolonged process time, unavailability of materials, 
and location of internal buffers [2].
Timely detection of process chain anomalies in 
Industry 4.0 environment is critical due to limited 
human intervention in the case of automated systems. 
These anomalies can be related to hardware, software, 
data, or communication. Variation in input signals 
received by the actuators, loss of input data, physical 
damage, or disruption in power can cause deviation of 
process parameters. These factors directly or indirectly 
affect the throughput of the process chain. The decrease 
in throughput due to bottleneck(s) is a pain point for 
every manufacturing process chain – assembly, produc­
tion, inspection, packaging – and requires swift and 
precise management.

The paradigms under the fourth industrial revolution 
offer a promising path to address the challenges faced 
by the manufacturing industry [3]. But the industry is yet 
to reach the maturity level for the wider application of 
industry 4.0 technologies to make them immune to 
disruption. Application of industry 4.0 enabling technol­
ogies through digital twins (DT), intelligent models, 
improved simulation techniques, and better visualiza­
tion have become imperative for better monitoring, 
control, visualization, and informed decision making 
[4]. The bottleneck management and anomaly detec­
tion methods require an integrated approach for cap­
turing the root causes and their impacts in the context 
of complex manufacturing systems. Therefore, in order 
to tackle the aforementioned challenges, this paper 
proposes a novel cognitive DT (CDT) approach.
DT is a digital representation of a physical asset that 
can be used to describe its behavior and characteristics 
by simulating the virtual model in the virtual space [5]. 
Penetration of DT solutions in industrial applications 
have been slow despite the increased interests in aca­
demic and industrial research [6]. This primarily may be 
due to the lack of availability of unified frameworks to 
support DT development. DT based methodologies for 
automated anomaly detection, self-diagnosis and 
response are relatively new but seems to be prospec­
tive tool [7]. For process improvement, DT is a highly 
efficient method by analyzing a product and its pro­
duction system across its life cycle [8].
Integration of cognitive models with manufacturing 
processes and systems supports the organizations to 
identify imbalances and to make informed decisions. 
Ahmed [9] defined CDT as an augmentation and intel­
ligent companion of physical system with its DT. CDT 
extend the traditional boundaries of model based 
approaches [10] by integrating the data driven predic­
tion models. Jiang et al. [11] in their study on cognitive 
manufacturing opined that the manufacturing system 
should have performance cognitive, optimal decision- 
making, real-time warning, knowledge learning, and 
reasoning learning. In this paper a CDT is proposed to 
detect, estimate, and predict the process chain bottle­
neck and serve as a decision support system for both 
operational and strategic decision making.
The future bottleneck prediction plays a key role in 
the efficient planning, scheduling, and control of man­
ufacturing process chains. The bottleneck prediction for 
the near future [12,13] has gained wider attention espe­
cially when dealing with the shifting bottleneck pro­
blem. Sustainability of artificial intelligence (AI) models 
can be achieved only by improving the capabilities of 
interpreting and explaining their results [14]. The 
explainable AI (XAI) models with DT bring the elements 
of transparency and justification along with the uncer­
tainty reduction in decision-making [15]. The explain­
ability makes the continuous improvement of models 
possible [16] and is important for maintaining the model 
effectiveness throughout the life cycle of the process 
chain. An XAI model – temporal fusion transformer 
(TFT) – is proposed and developed in this paper to 
predict future bottlenecks and interpret its results.
The key contributions (Figure 1) of the proposed 
cognitive digital twin methodology are:
(1) Detect the existing bottlenecks in the process 
chain.
(2) Detect data anomalies and process chain 
anomalies.
(3) Estimate the shifting bottlenecks due to the 
anomalies in the process chain.
(4) Predict and explain the future bottlenecks in the 
process chain using XAI model.
(5) An XAI model to support strategic and opera­
tional decision making.

2. Related literature
The section provides an overview of the existing litera­
ture on managing bottlenecks in process chains and the 
concepts of CDT and XAI in the manufacturing industry.
2.1. Bottleneck management
The initial work on bottleneck identification in produc­
tion lines was presented by Kuot et al. [17] in which an 
information based bottleneck identification rule for 
a serial production line was presented. The analysis of 
the data-driven bottleneck techniques reveals that 
numerous methods have been studied in literature for 
managing bottlenecks. Roser and Nakano [18] in their 
work compared six different bottleneck methods – cycle 
time and utilization, waiting time and queue lengths, 
arrow method, turning point method, active period 
method, and bottleneck walk. The bottleneck detection 
methods discussed in literature are tabulated (Table 1) 
with their relevance to the bottleneck detection state 
(current, future, shifting) and time (real-time, offline).
Bottleneck detection based on its state can be classi­
fied into current, future, and shifting bottleneck. The 
majority of the studies have proposed and developed 
methodologies – turning point method [20,31], 
hierarchical clustering [30], constrained resource identifi­
cation with factory physics [32] – for detecting the current 
bottleneck. Recent studies show that the detection of 
bottlenecks in real-time is important in an automated 
industry 4.0 production environment. Latsou et al. [36] 
proposed a multi agent cyber physical system for current 
bottleneck detection in real-time. The authors developed 
an agent-based simulation method to capture the prob­
abilistic variability and parallel dynamic interactions. A DT 
based framework to detect, diagnose and improve cur­
rent bottlenecks in real-time using utilization based tech­
nique was suggested by Kumbhar et al. [34].
Few studies have dealt with managing shifting bottle­
necks [25]. Fang et al. [28], proposed a methodology for 
detecting shifting bottlenecks while capturing temporal 
correlation and simultaneously depicting the production 
status through the parallel gated recurrent units’ model 
(p-GRUs). Future bottleneck prediction models have been 
developed with data driven techniques like neural net­
works [13,29], moving average – Auto Regressive Moving 
Average [21], Auto Regressive Integrated Moving 
Average [26] and Long Short Term Memory model [27].
Bottleneck detection based on its time of detection 
can be classified into offline and real-time. The majority 
of the studies have developed an offline method to 
detect bottlenecks or manage shifting bottlenecks. 
Most of the real time bottleneck management techni­
ques have been used to predict future bottleneck 
[12,13] with a few studies [34,35] detecting current 
bottleneck in real-time.

2.1.1. Anomaly detection
Industry 4.0 technologies support the continuous 
monitoring of process chains, enabling the detection 
of deviations of the system from its intended working 
characteristics. The anomaly may be due to a range of 
factors, but if left undetected/unattended the system 
performance gets affected. Chiang et al. [37] pre­
sented one of the initial works for bottleneck detec­
tion considering unreliable machine as anomaly. 
Anomaly detection is critical in automated process 
chains, and some studies have been conducted on 
detection and management [29,35] of process chain 
anomalies leading to improvement in process chain 
throughput. An anomaly detection approach with 1- 
Dimensional Convolutional AutoEncoders (1d-CAE) 
and Localized reconstruction error (LRE) to improve 
anomaly detection performance and interpretability 
was proposed and demonstrated by Gorman et al. 
[38]. The anomaly detection techniques in industry 
4.0 environment are primarily based on the monitor­
ing of field data with suitable IoT technologies. 
However, the implications of real-time anomaly 
detection and its impact on the throughput of 
a complex process chain in an Industry 4.0 environ­
ment have not been extensively addressed.
From the given Table 1, it becomes apparent that 
the referenced studies have focused on the detection 
of current bottlenecks through offline methods. The 
prediction of future bottleneck has been through 
“Blackbox” [39] models with no interpretation of 
results. Limited studies have addressed the issue of 
anomaly detection and shifting bottleneck(s) due to 
an anomaly. Based on the literature background the 
desired features of a bottleneck management 
methodology are compiled and depicted in Figure 2. 
Since bottleneck analysis is a crucial step in attaining 
increased production, it cannot be approached par­
tially as it significantly impacts the overall outcome of 
the process chain. There exists a major gap in the 
literature for a comprehensive bottleneck manage­
ment approach that can effectively detect existing 
bottlenecks, estimate the potential bottleneck shift 
due to anomalies, and predict future bottlenecks. The 
listed features act as the guide for developing the 
proposed CDT based bottleneck management metho­
dology. The proposed CDT is capable of detecting 
existing, shifting as well as future bottlenecks and the 
proposed XAI model is capable of supporting the 
operational and strategic decision-making.
2.2. Cognitive digital twins in manufacturing
The primary objective of industry 4.0 based manufactur­
ing systems is to attain self-sufficiency, requiring the 
ability to adapt to dynamic circumstances. A CDT can 
be defined as an enhanced version of a digital twin that 
possesses cognitive capabilities, allowing it to perceive 
and respond to unforeseen situations, resulting in 
a constantly evolving system [40]. CDT incorporate cog­
nition models through machine learning and artificial 
intelligence [41] which can effectively acquire essential 
cognitive aspects such as attention, perception, mem­
ory, reasoning, learning, and problem solving [42]. 
Development of CDT becomes important to handle 
the dynamic manufacturing systems and support resi­
lience in resulting production disruptions [43]. Figure 3 
depicts the building blocks of a CDT. An efficient CDT 
model provides the advantages of dynamic perception,

2.2.1. Explainable AI in manufacturing
The data analysis techniques have been revolutionized 
by the remarkable capabilities of data-driven AI 
(Machine learning, Deep learning, & AI) models. 
However, these models have faced criticism due to 
their lack of transparency and trustworthiness in practi­
cal implementation, hindering effective decision mak­
ing. As a result, XAI techniques have been developed to 
enhance the transparency of these models [44]. The 
primary focus of the XAI architecture for manufacturing 
applications was on the development of supervised 
learning-based classification and regression models. An 
instance of this is the creation of a two-stage XAI classi­
fication model specifically designed for predicting cycle 
time [45]. Notably, the XAI model developed for unsu­
pervised learning cluster analysis also has showcased its 
ability, like the model to analyze multidimensional pro­
totype clusters by providing rule-based explanations 
that are easily understandable to humans [46].
Various XAI methods – SHAP [16], class activation 
map and Gradient saliency maps [39] – have been 
explored and demonstrated. The time-series methods 
include based on the taxonomy of time points, sub­
sequences – sub-parts of the time series, as well as 
instance-based approaches [47]. Lim et al. [48] intro­
duced the TFT interpretable model. The architecture is 
based on the attention network combining high- 
performance multi-horizon forecasting with interpre­
table insights into temporal dynamics. Sanit and 
Toahchoodee [49] demonstrated the application of 
TFT model by implementing a discrete event simula­
tion and demand forecasting for validating the spare 
parts inventory policy for a petrochemical industry.
The application of XAI in manufacturing is still in its 
initial stages. However, its ability to interpret and 
provide explanations for the outcomes of AI models 
holds great promise as a potential solution for wider 
implementation of AI models in manufacturing 
industry.
2.3. Research gap
Based on the aforementioned literature, it is evident 
that the following disparities exist between the current 
status and requirements.
●Prediction of future bottlenecks and their inter­
pretation through decision support systems 
needs further investigation.
●The analysis of the implications of real-time 
anomaly detection and its impact (potential shifts 
of bottlenecks) on the throughput of a complex 
process chain in an Industry 4.0 environment has 
been lacking in the literature.
In order to bridge these disparities, this paper presents 
a CDT approach based on the bottleneck management 
desired features (Figure 2), that can proficiently manage 
prevailing, shifting, and forthcoming bottleneck situa­
tions. The CDT is constructed by combining a TFT-based 
interpretable AI model with a discrete event simulation- 
based DT. The DT enables the detection of anomalies, 
both in the process as well as the data. Meanwhile, the 
interpretable transformer based TFT model forecasts the 
upcoming bottleneck station and elucidates the key 
factors supporting the predicted outcome.

3.2. Cognitive digital twin
Development of DT requires majorly two components: 
simulation model and real time process data. The static 
part of the simulation model is developed based on 
process chain layout information. The layout informa­
tion provides the physical parameters like the distance 
the workpiece has to travel between stations, sensor 
and actuator positions, material handling units routing, 
and the location of buffer storage and retrieval station. 
The dynamic part of the simulation model is developed 
based on the process logic without deviating from the 
sequence of operation. The process parameters like 
speed of the conveyors & material handling units, 
number of workpieces allowed in a station at 
a particular instance are integrated with the model. 
The physical process chain data/information in some 
scenarios can be dynamic, leading to the requirement 
of self-configuring simulation models, depending upon 
the maturity level of the solution. The configured data­
base acquires the live data from the process chain. The 
required data to develop the DT of the process chain is 
selected and preprocessed to restrict the error in model 
due to data discrepancy. The preprocessed data is inte­
grated with the simulation model to develop the DT of 
the process chain. The developed DT model is tuned to 
synchronize with the physical process chain.
Figure 6 shows the DT development and validation 
process. The validation process of DT model involves 
examination of the accuracy and reliability in replicat­
ing the real system’s functioning and performance [50]. 
The validation of DTs is a crucial step to ensure the 
trustworthiness of the results for decision making. The 
procedure initiates with the validation of the simula­
tion model, followed by the data validation. Several 
quantitative methods have been discussed in the lit­
erature [51] supporting the validation of DTs. 
Performance value comparison is one of the most pre­
ferred methods of model validation where the error 
percentage of the performance criteria are taken into 
account [52]. According to ISO 23,247 standards for 
digital twin, the error percentage for synchronization 
is not fixed but depends on the application for the 
information exchange between the physical process 
chain and its digital twin [53]. Understanding the 
requirements of the DT, credibility assessment, and 
identifying the potential uncertainty areas of the DT 
[54] helps in developing an effective validation frame­
work and define the basic rules [55] that are needed to 
be satisfied throughout the lifecycle of the DTs.

3.2.1. Explainable AI model
Predictive models have found widespread applications in 
the manufacturing industry due to their ability to provide 
insights for taking critical decisions. The data-driven AI 
models are trained and validated to work with DT for 
better decision making. But the general drawback among 
these models is the opaqueness, referred to as the 
Blackbox model [46]. This opaqueness hinders the deci­
sion-making factor and constraints the model from being 
used practically. The explainable AI models provide the 
capability of interpreting and explaining the reasons for 
the predictions made by the model. This helps in under­
standing the critical parameters based on which the pre­
dictions are made. The AI model can be developed based 
on neural network or reinforcement learning. Figure 7 
depicts the flow of data in the cognition model to predict 
and interpret the bottleneck process. The process of 
development of the XAI model are as follows:
Step 1: Preprocess the data for training, testing, and 
validating the prediction model.
Step 2: The prediction length, lookback value and 
the training set split value is defined.
Step 3: The training set is developed with the para­
meters timestamp id, target variable, minimum enco­
der length, maximum encoder length, minimum 
prediction length, time varying unknown variables, 
target scales, encoder length.
Step 4: The validation and test datasets are split 
with the parameter of randomization. In this study 
the randomization is set to be true.
Step 5: From the developed datasets the data- 
loaders are generated by providing the batch size.
Step 6: The stop criteria is defined to save time. 
During the model training if the criteria is satisfied 
the current epoch will stop and proceed for the next 
epoch. Also, the trainer module is developed with the 
controller configuration, callback configuration, num­
ber of epochs. The trainer module is used to train and 
optimize the model further.
Step 7: The trainer module is used to fit and train the 
data from the training dataset. The trained model is 
tested for its accuracy by predicting the target variable 
for the input features from the test set. On getting the 
satisfactory results the model is validated against the 
validation dataset.

3.3. Anomaly detection
The robustness in synchronizing the data with the simu­
lation model decides the effectiveness of the DT. Thus, 
continuous data must be ensured from the source to the 
model. The validated DT provides the real time status of 
the physical model and process chain parameter insights 
from the field data. Early detection of errors and the 
deviation trends from the required performance criteria 
save production time and resources. Delay in receipt or 
non-receipt of data due to a broken channel will be 
notified as an anomaly and an alert information will be 
generated (Figure 8). The anomaly in the system may be 
due to data errors, measurement uncertainties, modeling 
errors or result interpretation errors. The validation 
process of DT helps to negate the modeling and result 
interpretation errors. The system should validate the 
receipt of data from field devices on a real time basis to 
confirm the synchronization of the digital model.
The monitoring of the working parameters and the 
insights gained from the data help in maintaining the 
system to function at the desired level. The fault in the 
physical process chain components may produce pro­
cess parameter results which are not in the acceptable 
range. Deviation of results from the desired values will 
be flagged as an anomaly and notified to the user. 
Identifying and notifying the anomaly condition is 
important but is not sufficient as the effects due to 
the anomaly also should be analyzed. The bottleneck 
of the process chain may start shifting due to the 
anomalous value. This becomes critical in systems 
where the anomalous value is generated over 
a period and not as an instantaneous spike.

blockage time plus starvation time compared to

future scenarios. The anomaly in the process 
chain can be due to the anomalous data from 
the physical device or due to the anomalous 
working of devices which are not detected as 
fault. The bottlenecks, due to the dynamic nature 
of the manufacturing process chain or due to an 
anomaly may shift location. The shifting bottle­

is due to the continuous deviation from the 
desired value, spread over several cycles. The 
“active period method” is ideal for simulation-

ease of implementation, and not needing infor­

also have a high level of confidence [57].
(3) Predict near future bottleneck – predict future

detecting existing bottlenecks, (ii) estimating bottle­

In this study three models − 1-Dimensional convolu­

●1D CNN – It is a convolutional neural network based 
DL method using multi-layered artificial neural net­
works to deliver high accuracy for applications such 
as object detection, speech recognition, language 
translation etc. The model is developed with three 
convolutional layers followed by a Max pooling and 
flattening layer. Rectified linear unit is used as the 
activation function. “Adam” optimizer is used for 
compilation and root mean square error (RMSE) is 
calculated as the error metric [60] to compare the 
results of the models.

time series analysis due to its inherent capability 
to capture long-term dependencies [61]. In this 
study a bi-directional LSTM model is developed 
with “Adam” optimizer and RMSE error metric.

based model utilizing self-attention to capture tem­
poral dynamics for time sequences. The TFT model 
was originally designed for capturing long 
sequences of data especially in applications invol­

architectural capability to process sequences of 
data, the model is widely researched for time series 
datasets. Additionally, the TFT model offers an inte­
grated interpretation mechanism that can calculate 
the significance of features in making predictions. 
The TFT model finds wide applications in manufac­
turing domain like fault detection and classification 
[63], tool wear state recognition [64], remaining 
useful life prediction of Industrial IoT systems [65], 
and also in other related domains such as for wind 
speed prediction with multivariate time series [66], 
and battery fault diagnosis and failure prognosis for 
electric vehicles [67].

5. Conclusions
Real time detection of bottlenecks in a process chain is 
key to improving the system productivity and through­
put. A cognitive digital twin has been presented to 
detect existing bottlenecks, estimate shifting bottle­
necks due to anomalies, and to predict and interpret 
future bottlenecks. The proposed methodology is 
demonstrated and validated through a didactic mod­
ular production system process chain (learning fac­
tory), mimicking the industrial environment.
A fully functional CDT of the process chain under 
experiment is developed using AnyLogic simulation 
tool and validated with performance value comparison 
method. The turning point method is used to detect 
the process chain bottlenecks and Active period 
method is used to detect the shifting bottleneck due 
to anomaly. Three models for near future bottleneck 
prediction – CNN based, LSTM based, and transformer 
based TFT model – are developed and compared. It 
was found that the transformer based TFT model pro­
vides better prediction results based on RMSE metric 
and has an integrated interpretation mechanism. 
Transformer model can capture both short-term 
changes and long-term trends. The model has obvious 
advantages in overcoming the interdependence pro­
blem of the influencing factors in long sequences [69]. 
However, comparison of CNN, LSTM, and Transformer 
models in predicting future bottlenecks needs to be 
carried out experimentally and needs further research. 
Optuna framework is used to optimize the hyperpara­
meters of the model, after determining the optimal 
learning rate. The variable selection module of the 
TFT model is used to estimate the feature importance. 
The optimized XAI model predicts the blockage and 
starvation times of all the stations and assists in detect­
ing the bottleneck station with the turning point 
method. The importance of the encoder, decoder, 
and attention index features are explained in the TFT 
model interpretation results. DT provides better con­
trol of the process chain and the TFT model predictions 
with its explanation help in decision making. The pro­
posed novel CDT methodology contributes to the 
existing body of literature in managing process chain 
bottlenecks (existing, shifting, and future) and support 
strategic and operational decision making.
Further application of the proposed CDT method is 
elaborated for detecting the new/modified layout bot­
tlenecks through virtual commissioning. The proposed 
XAI model to predict the bottlenecks for varying pro­
cess parameters can be extended to schedule the 
maintenance plan, redundancy level estimation, and 
higher production rate planning. The CDT bottleneck 
analysis for a complex process chain with data col­
lected from multiple sources and estimation of best 
possible facility utilization rate without creating bottle­
