Tao Wu, Jie Li, Jinsong Bao and Zhengkai Jin
College of Mechanical Engineering, Donghua University, Shanghai, China
ABSTRACT
Sustainable production is for the manufacturing industry to improve production efficiency and 
reduce energy consumption. Production system complexities, which cause bottlenecks, greatly 
impact process efficiency. However, the challenge lies in effectively utilizing sustainable produc­
tion process knowledge, which is often under-exploited, particularly when integrating this knowl­
edge with knowledge graphs for production bottleneck prediction. To solve the problem, this 
study presents a method for dynamic bottleneck prediction by integrating knowledge graphs and 
spatio-temporal models, leveraging underused production process knowledge. Initially, develop­
ing a sustainable production process knowledge graph effectively captures correlations between 
workstation states and external factors at production bottlenecks. Subsequently, a knowledge 
cross-fertilization module merges this correlation knowledge with spatio-temporal features, 
enhancing bottleneck prediction accuracy and reliability. Therefore, it efficiently predicts each 
workstation’s blockage and starvation, which has significance in identifying accurately future 
bottleneck workstations. A case study demonstrates that this method significantly reduces root- 
mean-square error by 7% to 42% in predicting overall system blockage and starvation metrics. 
Moreover, the mean-absolute-error of this method is within 4.5% over various time scales of 
30,60,90, and 120 minutes. Furthermore, these results confirm that the method introduced holds 
considerable practical significance in precisely identifying future workstation bottlenecks, offering 
novel tools and insights for enhancing production efficiency.

1. Introduction
Economic globalization, informatization, and the 
high-tech revolution have catalyzed the transition 
from an industrial to a knowledge economy, signifi­
cantly impacting human society. The enhancement of 
production system efficiency demand elasticity plays 
an important role in the field of manufacturing 
(Beauchemin 2023; Loghin et al. 2020). Currently, the 
competitiveness of small and medium-sized enter­
prises primarily hinges on production efficiency and 
quality. Imbalances in resource allocation and the 
presence of unstable factors at workstations, such as 
material supply, personnel allocation, equipment fail­
ure, and personnel fatigue, can lead to production 
delays and bottlenecks (Huang et al. 2019).
Sustainable production has become a crucial stra­
tegic direction for enhancing resource efficiency and 
reducing energy consumption (Tang 2019). Research 
on bottleneck identification and prediction is 
a significant component of sustainable production, 
providing early warnings for potential and emerging 
issues in the production process to support strategic 
business decisions (Nandakumar, Saleeshya, and 
Harikumar 2020). Within the domain of sustainable 
production, thorough analysis of production bottle­
necks is essential not only for streamlining production 
processes but also for driving improvements in pro­
duction efficiency and the efficient utilization of 
energy, inherently showcasing the sustainable devel­
opment potential of the production system (Shang, 
Liu, and Gong 2024; Tu and Zhang 2022). Therefore, 
analyzing these throughput bottlenecks is crucial for 
improving overall efficiency. To significantly improve 
the competitiveness of enterprises, great attention 
must be directed toward refining the production pro­
cess and ensuring its seamless and efficient operation 
(L. Li et al. 2011; Xue et al. 2022).
In this context, research on predicting production 
process bottlenecks is a critical factor for future pro­
cess improvement. For example, bottleneck predic­
tion can provide decision-making reference to 
managers (Rocha and Lopes 2022). They can

2. Related works
2.1. Production process bottleneck prediction
To further improve processes and increase productiv­
ity, some scholars have focused on identifying bottle­
necks in the production process (Yan et al. 2023). For 
instance (L. Li, Chang, and Ni 2009), proposed a short- 
term and long-term known turning point method 
(TPM) throughput bottleneck identification method 
using blockage and starvation as indicators, which 
solved the production constraint problem between 
the production line targets and the buffer content 
records (Nandakumar, Saleeshya, and Harikumar  
2020). proposed a Lean Six Sigma approach known 
as DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and 
Control) to address efficiency stability problems, spe­
cifically focusing on overall equipment efficiency and 
productivity improvement in the manufacturing sec­
tor. Tu and Zhang (Tu and Zhang 2022) proposed an 
aggregation-based analysis algorithm to identify bot­
tleneck states at production workstations and facili­
tate 
dynamic 
resource 
allocation 
within 
manufacturing processes. However, it has been 
observed that bottleneck locations dynamically shift 
due to a combination of factors in the production 
process. Therefore, bottleneck prediction for produc­
tion processes is an important consideration for the 
future improvement of processes.
Currently, bottleneck prediction research has 
achieved more significant applications in manufactur­
ing processes (L. Li et al. 2011). proposed an autore­
gressive moving average model (ARMA) based on

workstations, employing blockage and starvation as 
indicators (Subramaniyan et al. 2019). proposed an 
autoregressive integrated moving average model 
(ARIMA) based on time-series features for throughput 
bottleneck prediction in production processes with 
the active period as a prediction indicator. Xue (Xue

Nonetheless, the methods above have limitations, 
such as applying to production processes with low 
complexity and failing to offer a comprehensive 
dynamic analysis of factors considered in production

nel changes. Consequently, neural network methods

dynamic bottleneck prediction problem related to 
multiple jobs within a manufacturing system by 
employing a two-layer Long Short-Term Memory 
(LSTM) network based on high-dimensional historical 
shop floor data, with blockage and starvation as pre­
diction indicators. Chen (Chen et al. 2023) proposed 
a hierarchical recurrent neural network (RNN) model 
based on high-dimensional feature data to address 
the complex time-varying interdependencies among 
different factors in the production process.
However, the majority of existing studies focus on 
individual equipment states or specific processes in 
manufacturing. These studies typically analyze the bot­
tleneck indicators of individual objects in the production 
process in terms of temporal characteristics. Such an 
approach tends to neglect the interactions of global 
workstations in the spatial dimension. As a result, pre­
dicting bottlenecks for the whole production system 
remains a challenge when approached solely from the 
perspective of individual objects. To overcome this chal­
lenge, researchers need to consider the knowledge of 
production workstation correlation and combine the 
spatial and temporal features of the production system 
to predict bottlenecks from a global perspective.
2.2. Spatial and temporal features fusion
Graph structures possess a robust capacity to encapsu­
late extensive information and are widely employed in 
scenarios that encompass graph structures and node 
attributes. In various domains, including traffic flow pre­
diction and destination forecasting, graph neural net­
works have evolved into a fundamental technical tool. 
Unlike the aggregation approach, which assigns weights 
equally for neighboring nodes, attention mechanisms 
have become an important approach for sequence- 
based tasks nowadays. The uniqueness of this type of 
attention mechanism offers adaptive attention to fea­
ture data, yielding more pertinent features that exert 
a greater impact on the subsequent model’s perfor­
mance. Among them (Kovi et al. 2017), proposed 
a graph attention model (GAT), which enables the spe­
cification of distinct weights for neighboring modes, 
effectively addressing the challenge of modeling spatio- 
temporal correlations (Lai et al. 2023). proposed 
a Bottleneck spatio-temporal Attention Network 
(BSTAN) that can address the complexity of spatio- 
temporal modeling of manufacturing systems to achieve 
the bottleneck prediction task.
(Zhu et al. 2022) considered the spatial and tem­
poral characteristics of traffic along with the com­
bined impact of external factors and proposed 
a knowledge representation-driven method based 
on spatio-temporal graph attention method for pre­
dicting speed and flow indexes at different traffic 
network locations. Meanwhile (Qiao et al. 2023), pro­
posed a spatio-temporal attention graph network 
using fused geographic knowledge, which can realize 
the aggregation of spatial neighborhood features to 
solve the crop yield prediction challenge.
However, 
despite 
significant 
advancements 
achieved by these methods in various domains, exist­
ing approaches to production system bottleneck pre­
diction confront specific challenges and limitations.
In contrast, the current approach to production 
system bottleneck prediction primarily relies on pro­
duction data and places less emphasis on the knowl­
edge of the production system. The bottleneck status 
within a production system is also subject to mutual 
constraints among workstation nodes, encompassing 
factors such as the layout of the production line, the 
workflow, and the size of buffers. Therefore, this 
research utilizes spatio-temporal features and the cor­
related features associated with external production 
factors for dynamic production process bottleneck 
prediction. This paper integrates both production 
process data and the broader knowledge of the pro­
duction system, considering the interaction among

2.3. Research gap
Based on the above literature, there are several key 
research gaps in the field of production bottleneck 
prediction and spatio-temporal feature fusion, which 
pose significant obstacles to the improvement of pro­
duction efficiency in the sustainable production 
domain. These limitations mainly include:
●Existing research often overlooks the spatial 
interactions between overall workshop pro­
cesses, focusing instead on the status of indivi­
dual equipment or specific processes. The 
limitation of this approach lies in its inability to 
capture the global dynamic behavior of the pro­
duction system.
●Current reviews lack a comprehensive dynamic 
analysis of various factors such as layout, combi­
nation, and personnel changes during the pro­
duction process. These factors significantly 
influence the formation and evolution of produc­
tion bottlenecks but have not been adequately 
considered in existing research.
●The present approaches fail to adequately merge 
the knowledge of the production system with 
spatio-temporal data, leading to inaccurate pre­
dictions of production bottlenecks. This separa­
tion impedes a deep understanding of the 
overall performance of the production system.
The above research gaps indicate an urgent need in 
the current sustainable production field for a more 
precise and effective mechanism for the integration of 
production process data and knowledge for dynamic 
workshop bottleneck prediction methods. This study 
aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods 
and contribute to the field of sustainable production 
process optimization.

3.3.2. Spatio-temporal modelling method
Considering the influence of a single workstation’s 
bottleneck state on the interconnectedness and spa­
tial dynamics among other workstations in the sus­
tainable production process, the Graph Attention 
model (GAT) is chosen (Kovi et al. 2017). This model 
is well-suited for capturing the spatial topological 
relationships within the sustainable production pro­
cess and the embedded attributes of individual work­
stations. For example, the existence of abnormalities 
in the T pro of a process at a single workstation will 
increase the probability of occurrence and duration of 
bottleneck states at other neighboring workstations. 
The graph attention strategy used in GAT enables 
adaptive focus on critical embedded features, 
facilitating the dynamic acquisition of significant 
information from nodes at neighboring workstations 
for the target production process node.
The feature dimensions are fixed for the input time

used to obtain the sequence data with a fixed time

4. Experimental section
The 
production 
line 
for 
suits, 
serving 
as 
a representative example of textile and garment 
manufacturing processes, exhibits the following 
distinct characteristics, including workstation com­
plexity, spatio-temporal correlation of processes, 
and constant adjustments in production demands. 
Consequently, flexible sustainable production pro­
cesses are essential to address demand variability. 
These attributes inherent to the textile and apparel 
production process render the prediction of pro­
duction bottlenecks a formidable undertaking. To 
further validate the effectiveness of the sustainable 
production process bottleneck prediction method 
proposed in this paper, this study adopts the 
actual production process data (including produc­
tion line structure, buffer size, and production flow 
situation) of a domestic textile manufacturing 
enterprise as the research dataset. Within this data­
set, the blockage time and starvation time of 
different types of bottleneck workstations were 
selected as the prediction indices for the through­
put bottleneck. The performance of the prediction 
method proposed in this paper is compared and 
analyzed alongside other benchmark models. The 
reason for selecting these indices is based on the 
specific focus of our research, which is to predict 
throughput bottlenecks in sustainable production 
processes. Blockage and starvation are key factors 
that directly contribute to reduced throughput and 
operational inefficiencies.

6. Conclusion
To maximize sustainable production efficiency and mini­
mize environmental impact, this paper proposes 
a dynamic method for predicting manufacturing pro­
cess throughput bottlenecks, integrating sustainable 
production process knowledge and spatio-temporal 
features.
●This approach facilitates the forecasting of spa­
tio-temporal dynamic throughput bottleneck 
metrics from a comprehensive, systematic 
viewpoint.
●Unlike previous studies that predominantly 
focused on spatio-temporal features, the pro­
posed approach emphasizes the integration of 
external factors impacting manufacturing.
●The identification of future bottleneck workstations 
relies on the overall distribution of blockage and 
starvation.
●An analysis using a textile production case study 
shows that the proposed algorithm more effec­
tively predicts future blockage and starvation at 
each workstation compared to the benchmark 
model.
In the future, the focus of the research primarily 
lies in exploring the impact of dynamic intelligent 
modeling methods based on the production pro­
cess knowledge graph on the performance of pro­
duction bottleneck prediction, as well as whether 
constructing a multi-agent system to enhance the 
perception of workshop status and bottleneck pre­
diction capabilities contributes to the improve­
ment of the intelligent level of sustainable 
production. In addition, to research on the aspect 
of transferability, this model can accommodate 
diverse sectors and manufacturing settings, and 
when coupled with sophisticated data analytical 
methods, it can improve the precision of bottle­
neck forecasts.
