A B S T R A C T
In manufacturing systems, bottlenecks act as constraints that limit system throughput. Extensive efforts have
been made to detect and predict bottlenecks. Traditional bottleneck prediction methods predominantly utilize
time-series feature analysis, which is limited in capturing the dynamic spatial dependencies introduced by
production material flow. To address these limitations, we proposed a dual spatial temporal neural network
for dynamic bottlenecks (Dual-BDSTN) to learn the dependencies of temporal and spatial features dynamically.
In the temporal module, a gated recurrent unit combined with a self-attention mechanism is employed to
capture the time-evolving dynamics of temporal features related to machine status. In the spatial module,
a dynamic graph neural network is employed to learn spatial information affected by dynamic production
material flow and a cross-attention mechanism captures the effect of temporal features on spatial features.
Finally, gated recurrent neural networks are applied to capture the temporal trends of the temporal and spatial
features to predict future starvation and blockage for identifying bottleneck locations. Experimental results
demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms the best benchmark, achieving a 5.95 and 2.95 reduction
in root mean square error for predicting starvation and blockage times of overall machines in the production
system respectively (over 10%), with a 2.85% improvement in bottleneck prediction accuracy.
1. Introduction
In manufacturing and production systems, throughput bottlenecks
are critical points that can significantly restrict the capacity and ef-
ficiency of an operation, thereby impacting overall productivity and
performance (Kumbhar et al., 2023; Li et al., 2022; Mahmoodi et al.,
2022). A bottleneck occurs when the production material flow is
hindered at specific points along the production line, leading to lower
throughput. Identifying, analyzing, and addressing bottlenecks are
essential for enhancing operational efficiency, reducing costs, and
improving product quality and delivery times (Tang, 2024; Shang
et al., 2024). As a result, the study of throughput bottlenecks has
become a critical focus in production engineering and management
research (Kumbhar et al., 2023).
Throughput bottlenecks mainly involve aspects such as processes,
material flow, and equipment. The primary characteristic of a bot-
tleneck is that its processing speed is slower than both the upstream
supply rate and the downstream demand rate. This imbalance results in
workpiece accumulation (blockage) upstream and workpiece shortages

In recent years, studies have gradually focused on the impact of pro-
duction information on bottleneck prediction. Manufacturing systems,
being highly complex and dynamic, are influenced by both external and
internal factors, which can alter machine status and production line
processes, leading to shifts in bottlenecks. Due to the high-dimensional,
temporal, and spatial nature of production information, predicting
bottlenecks in such dynamic and complex environments presents sig-
nificant challenges. Traditional input–output models, such as ARMA (Li
et al., 2011), and ANFIS (Cao et al., 2012), are primarily used to
handle linear time series data. However, they are limited in addressing
the various high-dimensional and nonlinear factors (Lai et al., 2023;
Wu and Li, 2024; Zhang et al., 2024), such as machine status, buffer
levels, and labour skill. On the other hand, machine learning methods
can effectively capture these complex nonlinear relationships (Mozina
et al., 2008). Because the data changes dynamically during processing,
capturing these temporal dependencies through machine learning is
essential for accurate bottleneck prediction, as they provide valuable in-
sights into the evolving machine status and production processes, which
directly influence the location and movement of bottlenecks (Nguyen
et al., 2021; Pang et al., 2023). Two-layer LSTM (Lai et al., 2018)
takes future features as inputs for prediction. P-GRU (Fang et al.,
2020) divides features into main inputs and auxiliary inputs. LSTM-
DAM (Liu et al., 2023) considers the roles of different features in the
training stage with a dual attention mechanism. However, traditional
time-series methods overlook spatial dependencies. To address this, Ma
et al. (2023) introduced a machine learning approach that integrates
spatial and temporal insights. In addition to the high-dimensional and
temporal data dependencies, some data also have spatial dependencies
due to the interaction between upstream and downstream stations,
as shown in Fig. 1. TUSB-LSTM (Ma et al., 2023) and BSTAN (Lai
et al., 2023) extract spatial dependencies from all inputs. However,
they do not consider that temporal features related to independent
machine characteristics will affect spatial features and not vice versa.
Material flow between upstream and downstream stations is affected
by temporal incidents tied to individual machine characteristics. When
a machine breakdown disrupts material flow, it causes blockage or
starvation in upstream and downstream machines, as shown in Fig. 1.
Moreover, the graph network-based BSTAN (Lai et al., 2023) represents
stations and their features using a graph, ignoring the fact that tem-
poral features related to machine characteristics are just time-evolving
dynamics and lack spatial dependencies. When spatial processing is
applied to temporal features, it may weaken their temporal dependen-
cies. Additionally, spatial features are handled statically using a fixed
adjacency matrix, overlooking the dynamic changes driven by material
flow. As a result, spatial–temporal bottleneck prediction methods are
still in the early stages and face significant challenges in effectively
learning spatial features.
To address the aforementioned challenges, a dual spatial–temporal
neural network for bottleneck prediction (Dual-BDSTN) is proposed.
This model integrates a graph neural network with dual attention mech-
anisms to effectively capture both spatial and temporal dependencies.
To prevent the weakening of temporal features during spatial process-
ing, a dual module is designed to independently model temporal and
spatial features. To capture the temporal characteristics of the temporal
feature related to station status, a gated recurrent unit (GRU) and a
self-attention mechanism are leveraged to learn temporal dependencies,
which allows different features to play different roles in a learning
stage. To dynamically capture spatial features, a graph convolutional
network (GCN) with a material flow-based dynamic adjacency matrix
is employed to model the complex processing relationships. To consider
the effect of temporal features on spatial features, a cross attention
mechanism is served to gain the dynamic relationships between tem-
poral and spatial features, enhancing the expression of spatial features.
Additionally, a GRU is used to predict future blockage and starvation
trends by integrating embedded spatial and temporal features. Finally,
a bottleneck identification method is applied to identify bottlenecks
using the blockage and starvation time. The main contributions of this
paper are summarized as follows:
• We design the Dual Spatial-Temporal Neural Network (Dual-
BDSTN) for bottleneck prediction, featuring a dual-module archi-
tecture for decoupled processing. This model efficiently extracts
spatial and temporal features while preserving temporal feature
integrity during spatial processing compared to previous methods
and outperforms in bottleneck prediction.
• Leveraging a gated recurrent unit combined with a self-attention
mechanism, we dynamically adjust the role of temporal features
over time. This approach allows the model to capture complex
temporal dependencies more effectively than traditional methods.
• Unlike static spatial models, we integrate graph neural networks
with a dynamic adjacency matrix based on real-time material
flow, capturing machine-to-machine interactions in an evolving
manufacturing system. A cross-attention mechanism further en-
hances spatial feature representation by incorporating temporal
dynamics.
• Our model uniquely combines gated recurrent neural networks
to jointly analyze spatial and temporal features, allowing for a
deeper understanding of their interdependencies and improving
the prediction of bottlenecks in complex, dynamic manufacturing
environments.
The remainder of this paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews the
relevant studies on bottleneck prediction and recent progress in graph
neural networks regarding the interaction of spatial features. Section
Section 3 describes the proposed temporal–spatial neural network for
bottleneck prediction. Section Section 4 illustrates the application of
the proposed method in a case study of a production line and compares
its performance to other benchmarks. Finally, the paper concludes in
section Section 5.
2. Literature review
2.1. Bottleneck identification methods
Current research in production line optimization has focused on
improving the detection of bottlenecks, which are critical constraints
that disrupt the smooth flow of operations. Efforts in this area have
primarily converged on two main methodologies: simulation-based
methods (Thürer et al., 2022; Mahmoodi et al., 2024; Prabhu et al.,
2024) and analytical methods (Mahmoodi et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023).
Simulation methodologies have proven especially effective in as-
sembly production lines and manufacturing systems, offering a way
to identify transient bottlenecks in high-variety, made-to-order pro-
duction environments. Thürer et al. (2022) demonstrated the effi-
cacy of simulation in identifying transient constraints by developing
custom models that accurately capture the unique characteristics of
specific production contexts. However, simulation faces challenges in
handling complex interferences in manufacturing systems. The need
for accurate model creation, combined with the complexity of real-
world environments, limits the broader applicability of simulation-
based approaches. To address these limitations, analytical methods
have emerged as an alternative, analytical methods have emerged as an
alternative, leveraging real-world processing data to detect bottlenecks
more quickly and effectively. A variety of statistical methods, includ-
ing the arrow-based method (Chiang et al., 2000), the active period

tendencies. By analyzing historical data, algorithms are designed to
capture time-evolving dynamics for real-time bottleneck prediction.
2.2. Bottleneck prediction models
Predicting future bottlenecks is crucial for anticipating bottleneck
shifts and enabling proactive management. To predict future bottle-
necks in manufacturing systems, deep learning methods have been
employed to learn the temporal dependencies of historical data and
predict bottlenecks for the next period. Li et al. (2011) employed
the ARMA for time series analysis to predict throughput bottlenecks
in a manufacturing system. However, due to the high dimensionality
and complexity of the manufacturing data, the prediction accuracy
is low. Addressing the challenge of high-dimensional data, Cao et al.
(2012) proposed an Improved Adaptive Network-based Fuzzy Inference
System (ANFIS) to simplify the high-dimensional inputs for prediction.
Traditional methods did not deal well with the dynamic data for the
shifting bottleneck prediction. Lai et al. (2018) proposed a two-layer
long short-term memory network for bottleneck prediction to learn
non-linear manner and long-term temporal dependencies of data to en-
hance the prediction accuracy. Based on a large volume of production
data collected in a manufacturing system, Fang et al. (2020) divided
the data into main inputs and auxiliary inputs, and proposed a parallel
gated recurrent units (P-GRUs) method to capture the shifting bottle-
neck efficiently. However, neighboring stations will affect each other.
Previous deep learning methods have mainly focused on the temporal
trends of manufacturing data, neglecting the spatial relationships in
production line systems. Spatial dependencies impact performance by
showing how changes in one part affect others. Capturing these spatial
relationships improves model predictions, enhancing decision-making
and optimization in manufacturing.
Indeed, considering the spatial influence of the line layout and
integrating information exchange between neighboring stations can
significantly enhance data correlation, thereby improving prediction
accuracy. Ma et al. (2023) proposed a temporal unidirectional and
spatial bidirectional long short-term memory (TUSB-LSTM) which cap-
tures the spatial dependencies by bidirectional long short-term memory
(Bi-LSTM) and learns the time-solving dynamics by LSTM. However, Bi-
LSTM is not specifically designed to capture spatial features in complex
production systems, and GNN may be more powerful in the topological
network structure of processing relationships.
Researchers have recognized the importance of spatial relationships
between nodes, especially in areas such as traffic flow prediction.
By integrating spatial relationships into predictive models, it becomes
possible to better capture the interconnected nature of networks, re-
sulting in more accurate predictions and improved decision-making in
management and planning. GNN (Zeng et al., 2022), GCN (Wang et al.,
2023), GAT (Hu et al., 2023) and GraphSAGE (Mejía et al., 2022)
can establish spatial dependencies between nodes by hierarchically
aggregating neighbor information and applying nonlinear transforma-
tions. When combined with temporal networks, this approach enables
more effective spatiotemporal predictions. Therefore, Lai et al. (2023)
proposed a bottleneck spatial–temporal attention network (BSTAN) to
capture the spatial and temporal relationships. Compared to the meth-
ods that only capture temporal trends, they perform better. However,
they employ a static adjacent matrix to capture spatial dependence,
which has difficulty in understanding the patterns and dynamics.
Existing methods often employ graph neural networks (GNNs) with
a static adjacent matrix to aggregate features from all neighboring sta-
tions without considering temporal variations. Differentiating between
intrinsic equipment properties and factors that directly influence spatial
domain features is essential for predicting bottlenecks at specific sta-
tions accurately. Intrinsic equipment properties refer to characteristics
inherent to the equipment itself. Spatial factors affect how workstations
interact with each other within the spatial domain, influencing the flow
of materials and the occurrence of bottlenecks. Indeed, aggregating
all features spatially can overlook crucial nuances within the system,
leading to degraded prediction accuracy. To address this issue, a refined
method has been proposed that separates spatial and temporal features,
allowing for their distinct processing to improve model performance.
3. The proposed methodology
To improve the accuracy of throughput bottleneck prediction in
production lines, we propose a dual module method that effectively
captures both spatial and temporal dependencies. This approach aims
to enhance prediction performance by integrating these critical features
within the model. The proposed method comprises three modules:
spatial inputs and temporal inputs, the dual temporal–spatial model,
and bottleneck identification, as depicted in Fig. 2.
The data is divided into spatial and temporal components. Spatial
features represent information about machine processing states, which
are strongly affected by part delivery factors, such as starvation and
blockage. Temporal features pertain to machine performance-related
states, including factors such as breakdowns, and cycle time. In the spa-
tial module, the GCN with a dynamic adjacency matrix is employed to
pool spatial features between adjacent stations, while a cross-attention

3.3. Bottleneck identification
A bottleneck is a workstation that restricts the planned process-
ing scheme and obstructs timely delivery. By identifying these bot-
tlenecks, the manufacturing system can take appropriate actions to
improve its efficiency. Many previous analytical methods have been
leveraged to detect the throughput bottleneck, such as the arrow-based
method (Chiang et al., 2000), the active period method (Subramaniyan
et al., 2018), the maximum production load method (Cao et al., 2012)
and the turning point method. Unlike others that focus on the optimal
time in systems, The Turning-Point Method (TPM) (Li et al., 2009) is a
data-driven method for knowing the performance in the short term as
well as the long term. Its indicators help in understanding the system
dynamics and reflecting the materials flow in the production line,
allowing for proactive measures to be taken to mitigate or eliminate
these issues.
The turning-point method (TPM) is utilized to identify future bot-
tlenecks with its indicators which are predictive machine blockage
and starvation time. Blockage occurs when a station cannot transfer
completed jobs to the next buffer, while starvation happens when a
station lacks the required parts to continue processing and becomes

4.4. Comparison study between different methods
In the study, five different methods, ARIMA (Guerra et al., 2024),
LSTM (Lai et al., 2018), BSTAN (Lai et al., 2023), TUSB-LSTM (Ma
et al., 2023) and proposed Dual-BDSTN, are applied to predict future
starvation and blockage time which are employed to identify bottle-
necks by TPM. The performance of these methods is evaluated by the
MAE and RMSE, and their results are compared accordingly. As shown
in Table 3, this table outlines the MAE and RMSE values for each
method, allowing for a straightforward comparison of their predictive
precision. Lower MAE and RMSE values indicate higher prediction
accuracy. Based on the experimental results in Table 3, the Dual-BDSTN
method outperformed the other benchmarks in terms of prediction
accuracy for future starvation and blockage times. Taking the blockage
time prediction of machine M7 as an example, the proposed Dual-
BDSTN reduces the error by 8.94% in MAE and 5.26% in RMSE
compared to BSTAN. Additionally, it achieves an overall reduction
in starvation and blockage prediction errors, with decreases of 5.95
and 2.59 in RMSE (over 10%), respectively. This demonstrates that
the proposed method effectively captures both temporal and spatial
dependencies within the production system, leading to more accurate
bottleneck predictions. By jointly analyzing blockage and starvation
times, these bottlenecks are accurately identified through TPM.
Compared to ARIMA and LSTM models, the TUSB-LSTM model
shows a significant improvement in predictive performance. This en-
hancement is mainly due to its integration of a bidirectional long
short-term memory (BiLSTM) layer, which captures spatial correlations
in the data, improving prediction accuracy. However, BiLSTM is not
specifically designed to capture spatial dependencies, which limits its
ability to fully account for spatial relationships within the production
system. As a result, the TUSB-LSTM model performs worse compared
to BSTAN and Dual-BDSTN. This is because GNN (Graph Neural Net-
works) are better suited to effectively capture spatial relationships and
feature dependencies in complex production systems. Equipped with a
dynamic adjacency matrix, the Dual-BDSTN model employs a dynamic
approach to capture both spatial correlations and temporal dependen-
cies of features. The proposed Dual-BDSTN outperforms BSTAN. The
dynamic matrix allows the model to adaptively refine its representation
of spatial relationships and temporal patterns based on the characteris-
tics of the input data. Additionally, since some data are only related
to machine attributes and exhibit solely temporal dependencies, the
input data are divided into spatial and temporal features. These features
are processed through a dual-model approach, preventing the GNN
from indiscriminately aggregating features from adjacent machines and
ensuring that temporal features retain their time-evolving dynamics. In
contrast, the BSTAN model relies on a static matrix to capture spatial
correlations and temporal dependencies, which lacks the flexibility
to adjust to the shifting spatial relationships between neighboring
machines. This limitation reduces the effectiveness of BSTAN in dy-
namically representing the evolving spatial interactions influenced by

leveraging the spatial relationships and influences between adjacent
machines to their fullest extent.

The major limitation of this study is the lack of a real-world dataset.
However, in future work, we aim to address this by collecting real
production line data and optimizing the model to make it suitable for
predicting bottlenecks in actual production lines. The specific approach
is outlined as follows:
(1) For real-world validation, we collect real-world datasets to val-
idate the model. In the future, when enterprises undertake the
digital transformation of production lines, deploying appropriate
sensors allows for the precise collection of working time, star-
vation time, blockage time, down time, and product volumes
in the buffer. The sensors capture seven signals: request for
loading, loading completion, request for unloading, unloading
completion, the start and end of an abnormality (fault), and the
status of buffers. Working time is calculated as the difference
between the timestamps of the loading completion signal and
the unloading request signal. Starvation time is the difference
between the timestamp of unloading completion and the times-
tamp of the subsequent loading completion. Blockage time is the
difference between the timestamps of the unloading request and
the unloading completion. Downtime is the difference between
the start and end timestamps of an abnormality (fault). The
product volumes in the buffer area are recorded by tracking the
change time and the current quantity of products whenever there
is a change in product volumes within the buffer.
(2) For model optimization, we implement strategies to enhance
computational efficiency, accuracy, and scalability, ensuring ro-
bust performance in real-world applications. To address com-
putational challenges in large-scale production environments,
such as massive data volumes and real-time requirements, we
implement distributed training strategies, including data paral-
lelism, model parallelism, and pipeline parallelism. These ap-
proaches optimize resource utilization while maintaining model
accuracy and efficiency. Moreover, we rigorously analyze fea-
ture relationships in existing datasets and develop advanced
methods to improve model interpretability. This includes ex-
tracting deeper insights through techniques such as exploring
the causal relationships of features, hierarchical feature fusion
and so on, to uncover latent patterns. For scenarios with limited
production-side data, we leverage knowledge distillation and
transfer learning frameworks. By transferring domain-specific
knowledge from high-resource settings or pre-trained models,
we aim to refine the learning process and improve prediction
accuracy in low-data regimes.
(3) Bottleneck prediction can indirectly generate benefits for the
production, and it requires integration with subsequent opera-
tional improvements to establish a closed-loop system. Through
this combination can production line material flows be dynami-
cally optimized, driving measurable efficiency improvements.
5. Conclusions
In this paper, we propose a dual data-driven architecture for pre-
dicting future throughput bottlenecks identified by the Turning Point
method, which is based on starvation time and blockage time. The
proposed Dual-BDSTN model dynamically captures spatial and tempo-
ral dependencies to predict future starvation and blockage times. In
the dual models, the dynamic graph weight is designed to consider
the relationships between upstream and downstream processing tasks.
Attention mechanisms are employed to learn contextual information in
different periods. The cross-attention mechanism integrates the impact
of temporal features on spatial features. To evaluate the performance
of the Dual-BDSTN model, we compare it with benchmark models
including ARIMA, LSTM, TUSB-LSTM, and BSTAN. The experimental
results demonstrate that the proposed Dual-BDSTN model outperforms
other models in terms of MAE and RMSE metrics, fitting true values
and the accuracy of bottlenecks prediction. The key findings can be
summarized as follows:
(1) The proposed dual model. we distinguish between features in-
fluenced by both neighboring stations and those that exhibit
only time-evolving dynamics based on machine-specific prop-
erties. These features are then categorized into spatial inputs
and temporal inputs. To handle these inputs effectively, a dual-
model (Dual-BDSTN) is proposed. This framework includes a
spatial module and a temporal module. The spatial module uses
a dynamic adjacency matrix to process spatial inputs and capture
the dependencies between neighboring stations. Meanwhile, the
temporal module focuses on the temporal dynamics of the ma-
chine properties. By separating spatial and temporal processing,
the model can leverage both spatial and temporal dependencies,
ultimately improving the prediction accuracy of bottlenecks.
(2) The proposed Dual-BDSTN outperforms the benchmarks in terms
of MAE and RMSE, especially at M2, which handles more com-
plex processing tasks, compared to M7, which handles simpler
tasks. This suggests that the Dual-BDSTN model is particularly
effective in capturing the intricate spatial–temporal dependen-
cies in systems with more complex machine dynamics. Com-
pared to BSTAN, the dynamic adjacency matrix in Dual-BDSTN is
more effective in capturing spatial dependencies than the static
matrix used in BSTAN. This dynamic approach allows Dual-
BDSTN to adapt to changing spatial relationships, improving its
ability to represent the evolving interactions between machines.
Furthermore, Dual-BDSTN demonstrates better fitting to true
values and higher accuracy in bottleneck prediction, achieving
an accuracy of 85.71%, which is a 2.85% improvement over
BSTAN. This performance enhancement highlights the effec-
tiveness of the dynamic approach in improving the prediction
quality in complex production environments.
(3) The attention mechanism significantly improves the model’s
ability to capture context spatial–temporal dependencies, cross-
attention more effectively models the interaction between tem-
poral and spatial features, and the GRU module enhances tem-
poral trend learning.
