\paragraph*{Historical and layer-wise attention structure.}
Figures~\ref{fig:attn-temporal}--\ref{fig:attn-horizons} examine temporal context within the same supplementary mixed-disturbance analysis. Figure~\ref{fig:attn-temporal} covers all 30 historical windows. The normal, upcoming and ongoing averages remain broadly similar, while the upcoming-minus-normal panel resolves smaller shifts in the historical positions receiving attention. Its difference scale is much narrower than the absolute-weight scale, so the panel should not be read as a large change in temporal dependence. Figure~\ref{fig:attn-layers} separates the five encoder layers for upcoming samples and exposes their different geographic and semantic allocation patterns. Layer-specific weights do not measure the accuracy contribution of each layer.

Figure~\ref{fig:attn-horizons} compares the upcoming-minus-normal resource maps from three separately trained Start$\leq5/10/15$ mixed-disturbance checkpoints, with 96 samples per condition and checkpoint. The persistent structure and localized changes provide a descriptive view across onset settings. They neither trace propagation through physical time nor replace the distance-resolved recall comparison in Table~\ref{tab:delay-ablation-new}.
