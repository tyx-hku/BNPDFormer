\begin{abstract}
Throughput bottlenecks in human--robot collaborative (HRC) factories are dynamic, multi-resource, and sparse in time.
Operators need precision-first warnings of \emph{which} station will congest, \emph{when} congestion starts, and \emph{how long} it lasts---rather than retrospective detection alone.
We present \textbf{BNPDFormer}, a spatiotemporal Transformer for station-level bottleneck event forecasting on heterogeneous factory graphs.
Windowed operational features of machines, workbenches, gantries, AGVs, humans, and buffers are encoded with a PDFormer-style backbone (temporal, geographic, and delay-aware semantic attention).
The primary heads predict, for each supervised station over a fixed short horizon, a binary will-block flag together with start minute and duration; auxiliary heads regress remaining order completion time and a coarse process-cause class.
Labels are derived from unsupervised operational occupancy runs in an NVIDIA Isaac Sim replica of a modular-construction water-pipe line, under single-axis disturbances (machine, human, logistics, material).
On 134 episodes, BNPDFormer attains test report precision/recall/F1 of $0.817$/$0.447$/$0.578$ with start and duration MAE of $0.20$ and $1.32$\,min.
We compare against tabular and graph sequence baselines (XGBoost, LSTM, BTGCN, and BSTAN) under a shared protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}
%% ========================================================================

Modular construction (MC) shifts on-site work into production-part factories and increasingly adopts human--robot collaboration under Industry~5.0~\cite{gibb1999off,xu2021industry,fu2024human}.
On such lines, throughput is limited by shifting bottlenecks across machines, humans, logistics assets, and materials~\cite{roser2001practical,li2009data}.
Shop-floor decisions benefit from early, high-precision forecasts of \emph{where} a bottleneck will appear, \emph{when} it starts, and \emph{how long} it lasts.

Classical manufacturing research provides strong tools for bottleneck \emph{detection} from queue, process, and system states~\cite{roser2001practical,li2009data,subramaniyan2023throughput}.
These methods are primarily retrospective or short-horizon diagnostic.
In intelligent transportation systems, delay-aware spatiotemporal Transformers such as PDFormer forecast dense traffic states on graphs~\cite{jiang2023pdformer}, while related work studies sparse congestion events~\cite{jin2023stgnpp}.
Factory HRC lines share graph-like propagation but differ in heterogeneous resource types, process--logistics coupling, and the need for station-level event reports rather than road-flow maps.

We therefore ask: can a PDFormer-style encoder be adapted to emit precision-first station bottleneck events on a factory graph?
Our answer is \textbf{BNPDFormer} (Bottleneck Neural PDFormer), which:
\begin{itemize}
    \item encodes multi-resource window features on a process--logistics graph with temporal, geographic, and delay-aware semantic attention;
    \item predicts per-station will-block, start minute, and duration over a fixed look-ahead (15\,min given 30\,min history);
    \item jointly regresses remaining order time and a process-cause class as secondary outputs;
    \item is trained on disturbance-controlled Isaac Sim episodes with unsupervised operational occupancy labels.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:review} reviews related work.
Section~\ref{sec:problem} formalizes the forecasting task.
Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the data engine and BNPDFormer.
Section~\ref{sec:exp} reports experiments.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Literature review} \label{sec:review}
%% ========================================================================

\subsection{Bottleneck detection in manufacturing}
Throughput bottlenecks are resources that limit system output.
Detection methods are commonly grouped by queue state, process state, or system state information~\cite{subramaniyan2023throughput}.
Roser et al.\ identify bottlenecks via uninterrupted active periods~\cite{roser2001practical}.
Li et al.\ propose data-driven turning-point detection from blockage and starvation~\cite{li2009data}.
These approaches underpin diagnosis and improvement, yet they do not cast the problem as supervised station-level event forecasting with matched start-time tolerances on heterogeneous HRC lines.

\subsection{Spatiotemporal forecasting on graphs}
Spatiotemporal graph models and Transformers are widely used for dense traffic variables.
PDFormer introduces dynamic spatial attention, short-/long-range masks, and delay-aware feature transformation~\cite{jiang2023pdformer}.
Event-oriented traffic models further target sparse congestion timing~\cite{jin2023stgnpp}.
BNPDFormer retains a PDFormer-style encoder for factory graphs, but replaces dense flow heads with station event heads (will/start/duration) suited to shop-floor warnings.
We also compare graph sequence baselines that combine spatial operators (GCN or GAT) with temporal GRUs.

\subsection{HRC factories and simulation}
Industry~5.0 emphasizes human-centric manufacturing and HRC~\cite{xu2021industry,fu2024human,jahanmahin2022human,semeraro2023human}.
High-fidelity simulation (NVIDIA Isaac Sim) enables controlled multi-resource logs under disturbances that are costly to induce on real lines.
We use simulation as a labelled data engine for operational bottleneck forecasting from process and logistics states.

\subsection{Research gap}
Three gaps motivate this work:
(i)~manufacturing bottleneck literature is detection-heavy and rarely evaluates station event reports with precision-first matching;
(ii)~traffic forecasters assume homogeneous nodes and calendar periodicity;
(iii)~HRC factory datasets seldom couple process-consistent labels with controllable single-axis disturbances.
BNPDFormer addresses these gaps with an event-centric model and a disturbance-aware simulation pipeline.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}
%% ========================================================================

\subsection{Resources, graph, and windowed observations}
Let $\mathcal{R}=\{r_1,\ldots,r_N\}$ be heterogeneous resources (machines, workbenches, gantries, AGVs, humans, buffers).
A process--logistics graph $G=(\mathcal{R},E)$ encodes upstream/downstream process links, buffer affinity, and logistics attachments.
Time is discretized into windows of length $\Delta=60\,\mathrm{s}$.
At window $t$, each resource $n$ has a feature vector $x_{t,n}\in\mathbb{R}^{F}$ aggregating queue, process, starvation/blockage, logistics delay, material shortage, and disturbance indicators, concatenated with a resource-type one-hot.
An observation tensor over the past $T_{\mathrm{in}}$ windows is
\begin{equation}
X_t = \{x_{\tau,n}\}_{\tau=t-T_{\mathrm{in}}+1}^{t},\; n=1,\ldots,N \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\mathrm{in}}\times N\times F}.
\end{equation}
We use $T_{\mathrm{in}}=30$ (30\,min history) and a fixed forecast horizon $H=15$ windows (15\,min).
Humans and buffers provide context features; supervised forecast targets are stations in $\{\mathrm{machine}, \mathrm{workbench}, \mathrm{gantry}, \mathrm{AGV}\}$.

\subsection{Station-level bottleneck events}
For each supervised station $n$ and time $t$, the primary targets over the next $H$ minutes are
\begin{equation}
(w_{t,n},\, s_{t,n},\, d_{t,n}),
\end{equation}
where $w_{t,n}\in\{0,1\}$ indicates whether a bottleneck run occurs in the horizon,
$s_{t,n}\ge 0$ is the start offset in minutes (forced to $0$ if congestion is already ongoing),
and $d_{t,n}>0$ is the duration in minutes (remaining duration if ongoing).
A prediction is counted as a correct \emph{report} only if the station identity matches and $|\hat{s}-s|\le 3$\,min.
Secondary targets include remaining order completion time $\ell_t$ (minutes) and a process-cause class $c_{t,n}$ among six operational reasons (material shortage, transport delay, queue buildup, blocked downstream, starved upstream, high utilization).

\subsection{Learning objective}
Given encoder states $z=\mathrm{Enc}(X_t,G)$, BNPDFormer minimizes a weighted sum of event will/start/duration losses, light occupancy-grid auxiliaries for alignment, remaining-time regression, process-cause classification, and a small contrastive term on grouped embeddings.
Model selection uses validation report F1 with a precision gate (preferring high precision over aggressive recall).

\section{Research method} \label{sec:method}
%% ========================================================================

\subsection{Simulation data engine}
We reconstruct a water-pipe production-part line in NVIDIA Isaac Sim (storage, gantry/AGV logistics, cutting/grooving/welding/painting, workbenches).
A rule-based controller executes process and logistic tasks.
Each training episode orders $10$ pipes with WIP cap $5$; a no-disturbance reference makespan is about $133$\,min.
Raw logs record resource transitions, job traces, buffers, transport tasks, inventory, and disturbance injections.

\paragraph{Controlled disturbances.}
Formal training uses intensity $I=1.0$ and \emph{one} primary dimension per run: machine, human, logistics, or material (plus scarce no-disturbance controls).
Configuration changes (e.g., fewer workers or AGVs, closed stations) combine with episodic shocks (faults, leave events, freezes, temporary kit shortages).
Mixed dimensions are reserved for out-of-distribution evaluation and are not merged into training.

\paragraph{Window aggregation and labelling.}
Heterogeneous raw states are normalized and aggregated into per-resource window statistics.
Bottleneck events are unsupervised operational occupancy runs of at least eight consecutive hot minutes on supervised stations (AGV hotness follows freeze/inbound-wait criteria rather than ordinary travel delay).
Exported tensors exclude supervision-only scores from inputs to avoid label leakage.
Episodes are split at the episode level (no adjacent-window leakage across train/val/test).

\subsection{Graph construction}
Edges connect process neighbours, buffers to affiliated stations, logistics assets to served machines, and weak same-type links.
Adjacency yields geographic attention masks and Laplacian positional encodings.
Semantic neighbourhoods use behavioural correlation; short delay motifs support delay-aware geographic attention (DFT-style patterns).

\subsection{BNPDFormer architecture}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h &= \mathrm{Embed}(X_t)+\mathrm{PE}_{\mathrm{time}}+\mathrm{LapPE}(G),\\
z &= \mathrm{STEncoder}^{(L)}(h;\, G_{\mathrm{geo}}, G_{\mathrm{sem}}, \mathrm{DFT}),\\
&\quad (\hat{w},\hat{s},\hat{d}) = \mathrm{EventHead}(z),\\
&\quad \hat{\ell} = \mathrm{RemainHead}(z),\quad \hat{c}=\mathrm{CauseHead}(z).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Calendar day/week embeddings from traffic PDFormer are removed.

\paragraph{Shared encoder.}
Grouped token embeddings with type one-hots feed stacked spatiotemporal blocks: temporal self-attention per node, geographic attention over process--logistics neighbours with delay patterns, and semantic attention over behaviourally similar nodes.

\paragraph{Event heads.}
For each supervised station, a will-block classifier and start/duration regressors cover the $H=15$ horizon.
Ongoing congestion forces start targets to zero and trains duration as remaining length.
Occupancy grids remain as light auxiliaries only; they are not the checkpoint metric.

\paragraph{Auxiliary heads.}
Remaining order time is a scalar regression.
Process causes use six operational classes derived from window evidence (not the disturbance injection name).

\subsection{Baselines}
Under the same windows, split, labels, and report metrics, we compare:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{XGBoost}: flattened history features with multi-head tabular predictors;
    \item \textbf{LSTM}: non-graph sequence model with shared event heads;
    \item \textbf{BTGCN}: two-layer GCN spatial mixing followed by a GRU temporal encoder and event heads (GCN+GRU);
    \item \textbf{BSTAN}: multi-head GAT spatial mixing followed by a GRU and event heads (GAT+GRU).
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiments} \label{sec:exp}
%% ========================================================================

\subsection{Setup}
We train and evaluate on Isaac Sim episodes with $60\,\mathrm{s}$ windows, history $30$, horizon $15$, and intensity-$1.0$ single-axis disturbances.
The usable pool contains $134$ episodes (split $92/17/25$ for train/val/test), with $N=38$ graph nodes and $F=27$ input features.
Checkpointing maximizes validation report F1 subject to a precision preference (thresholding around $0.65$ on will-block).
Primary metrics are report precision/recall/F1 (station match and start error $\le 3$\,min), plus start/duration MAE, who-only F1, remaining-time MAE, and ongoing vs.\ upcoming recall.

\subsection{Main results}
Table~\ref{tab:main} summarizes test performance.
BNPDFormer reaches report precision $0.817$, recall $0.447$, and F1 $0.578$, with start MAE $0.20$\,min and duration MAE $1.32$\,min.
Remaining-order MAE is $6.27$\,min.
Who-only F1 is $0.581$, nearly matching report F1, indicating that most who-correct predictions also satisfy the start tolerance.
Ongoing events are recovered more readily than upcoming ones (report recall $0.579$ vs.\ $0.320$), which remains the main failure mode.

Baseline formal scores under the shared protocol are being finalized; architectures and training recipes are aligned with BNPDFormer (same data pack, split, and report metric).

\subsection{Discussion}
Precision-first selection yields usable station warnings with sub-minute start errors on matched events, at the cost of incomplete upcoming recall.
Auxiliary occupancy metrics (grid hot F1 $\approx 0.61$ on test) support alignment but are not used for checkpointing.
Process-cause accuracy is high in aggregate but class support is uneven; we therefore treat cause as a secondary analysis signal rather than a headline claim.
Future work will complete baseline numbers, strengthen upcoming-event recall, and evaluate mixed-dimension OOD settings under the same report protocol.

%% ========================================================================
\section{Conclusion} \label{sec:conclusion}
%% ========================================================================

We formulated factory bottleneck forecasting as station-level will/start/duration prediction on a heterogeneous process--logistics graph and introduced BNPDFormer, a PDFormer-style spatiotemporal encoder with event heads for HRC production lines.
A disturbance-aware Isaac Sim data engine supplies unsupervised operational labels.
On 134 episodes, BNPDFormer achieves report F1 $0.578$ with high precision and tight start timing; upcoming-event recall and completed baseline comparisons remain priorities for deployment-ready warnings.
